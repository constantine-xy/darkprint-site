Privacy Policy

Effective 17 October 2026

Darkprint develops photos and videos on your iPhone. It has no account, sends nothing anywhere, and collects no data about you.

At a glance

  • Nothing leaves your iPhone. The app has no account, no analytics, and never contacts the internet.
  • Your originals are always kept. A photo's edit is saved into the photo the way Photos' own edits are, so Revert in Photos brings the original back. Videos and collages are saved as new copies.
  • The app asks for Full Access to Photos to show your library and save your edits. It never deletes or hides anything in it.

Your photos and videos

  • Everything happens on your iPhone. Developing, previews and exports run on the device. Nothing is uploaded or processed on a server. When a photo or video is stored only in iCloud, Photos downloads it from your own iCloud account for the app to develop; nothing goes anywhere else.
  • Photos keeps every original. A photo's edit is saved into the photo as an edit, as the Photos app's own Edit does: Photos keeps the original, Revert in the Photos app brings it back, and Darkprint can reopen its edit later. The edit is stored with the photo as Darkprint's adjustment data, the look and settings you chose; with iCloud Photos, Photos syncs it along with the photo, as it does any edit.
  • Videos and collages are saved as new copies. A developed video or a collage goes into your library as a new item, and the original stays as it was.
  • Pictures from outside Photos are added to Photos first. A photo or video you bring in from Files, the clipboard, drag and drop or another app is added to your library as it is, and the app says so; then it opens like any other.
  • What the app keeps. The Darkroom keeps your drafts and a record of what you developed on this iPhone, along with the extra pictures some edits need: a double exposure's second photo, a collage's photos, and the picture another app's edit made. These stay in the app's private storage and are included in your iPhone's iCloud Backup, so those edits can reopen after a restore. Working copies of library photos are kept in a cache that iOS can clear. Deleting the app deletes all of it; your photos, and the edits saved into them, stay in Photos.

Photos access

  • Darkprint asks for Full Access once, in its welcome, after a card that says why. It uses it to show your library in the app, to save each photo's edit into the photo, to add new copies and the pictures you bring in, and to find its own edit again when you reopen a photo.
  • With Limited Access, the app shows and edits only the photos you picked, and you can pick more at any time. Without access, the app says so and can open iOS Settings.
  • It never deletes, hides or moves anything in your library, and never changes a photo except to save the edit you made in it.
  • You can change this permission at any time in iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Photos.

Metadata

A photo or video may carry metadata such as the date it was taken, the exposure and a location. The app reads it for features like the date stamp and the camera bar. An edit saved into a photo keeps the photo's own metadata, location included, as edits in Photos do. A new copy carries metadata only as your export options say, and its location only when you choose Include Location.

What the app doesn't do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No analytics, crash reporting or tracking of any kind.
  • No advertising and no third-party code.
  • No network connection. The app never contacts the internet.

Children

The app is suitable for all ages and collects no data from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published on this page with a new effective date.
